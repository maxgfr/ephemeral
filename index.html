Two tools on one small core.

The room

For talks, classes and meetings. Your screen shows a QR code; the audience joins on their phones and sends reactions, votes in your polls and asks questions others can upvote. You stay the only one who can open a poll or publish a result.

  • One click to create, nothing to install
  • Polls with two to four options, live bars
  • Q&A sorted by votes, mark answered, hide
  • Press F for a clean projector view

The widget

For blogs, docs and any static page. Paste one tag and readers see how many people are on the page with them, can send a reaction everyone sees float by, and spot where the others are in the text. Alone on the page, it stays quiet.

  • One <script> tag, no build, no key
  • Shadow DOM: your CSS and ours never meet
  • One room per page, the URL is hashed
  • Fails silently if a network blocks it

What it can't do.

    
No server means no server's guarantees. These are trade-offs of the approach, stated up front.

    

      
Peers see each other's IP address
WebRTC connects browsers directly, so everyone in a room or on a page can see the others' IP addresses. On a professional site, tell your readers; this counts as personal data under GDPR.

      
A few dozen people, not five hundred
Every peer connects to every other. Thirty people means 435 connections, and phones start to struggle past a few dozen. Good for a meetup or a class, wrong for a keynote.

      
Some connections fail
Without a TURN relay, strict NATs and some corporate, school or mobile networks block direct connections. Expect roughly one person in ten to stay on "Connecting".

      
Nothing is kept
When everyone leaves, it is gone. On a quiet site the widget is mostly alone and stays hidden. The presenter's browser saves the room state locally so a reload doesn't wipe it.

      
Votes can be gamed
Reactions and votes are anonymous. Someone determined can reconnect and vote again. Rate limits slow abuse down; they don't stop it. Don't use it for a vote that matters.

      
Relays belong to others
Signaling uses public Nostr relays run by third parties. Several are tried at once, but they can be slow or go away.
